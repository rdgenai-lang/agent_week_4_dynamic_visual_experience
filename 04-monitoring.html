AI System Monitoring

Catching silent failure before your users do

A traditional bug crashes loudly. An AI model fails quietly — the latency is fine, the API returns 200 OK, but the answers slowly get worse. Monitoring AI in production is about three things: noticing the drift, scoring the quality, and being paged before things get bad.

1
Concept One
Prompt Drift — when reality changes under your model
1/3 Walkthrough

The world your model knew

When you launched, you tested your AI against the prompts you expected: password resets, billing questions, the usual. Performance was great. Everyone celebrated. Then time passed.

Key ideaA model's accuracy on launch day is a snapshot, not a guarantee. The world drifts; the snapshot doesn't.
2
Concept Two
Response Quality — measuring what "good" means
1/3 Walkthrough

"Was that a good answer?"

For traditional software, monitoring is straightforward — did it return 200 OK, how fast? For AI, the response can be lightning fast and confidently *wrong*. Quality is the metric that matters most and the hardest to measure automatically.

Key ideaLatency tells you if your AI is alive. Quality tells you if it's still useful.
3
Concept Three
Alerting on Degradation — getting paged before users notice
1/3 Walkthrough

Dashboards don't watch themselves

Pretty charts on a dashboard only help if someone is looking at them. Real monitoring means defining sharp, numeric thresholds — "if accuracy drops below X for Y minutes" — that automatically page a human. You decide the rules once; the system watches forever.

Key ideaDon't ask 'is the system okay?' Ask 'what specifically would tell me it isn't?' Then alert on that.
